แบบขอมผสมไทยโบราณ

[ยันต์: แผนภาพสี่เหลี่ยมจัตุรัส มีดอกจันหกจุดเล็กๆ ประจำอยู่ที่มุมทั้งสี่ ตัวสี่เหลี่ยมถูกลากเส้นทแยงมุมทั้งสองเส้นและมีรูปหกเหลี่ยมดาว (ยันต์ตรีเพชร) ซ้อนอยู่ภายในแบ่งเป็นช่องย่อย มีเลขไทย ๑ ถึง ๘ เขียนไว้ในแต่ละช่องตามแนวทแยง มีอักขระขอมเรียงเป็นแถวอยู่นอกกรอบด้านบน ด้านล่าง และด้านข้างทั้งสองข้างของสี่เหลี่ยม และมีอักขระขอมสั้นๆ อยู่ในดอกจันแต่ละมุม — [อักขระขอมอ่านไม่ชัด]]

Khom Script Mixed with Ancient Thai Style

[yantra: a square diagram with a small six-dot rosette at each of the four corners; the square is crossed by both diagonals and has a six-pointed star (interlocking triangles) inscribed within it, dividing it into compartments; Thai numerals 1 through 8 are written diagonally in the compartments; khom-script inscriptions run in a line outside the top edge, bottom edge, and both side edges of the square, with short khom syllables inside each corner rosette — [illegible: the khom characters cannot be read with confidence]]

The formula for yantra inscription, khom-mixed ancient Thai edition. Compiling more than 200 yantras. Together with a primer for writing khom script. Translating incantations from Thai into khom. Every yantra design is accompanied by its consecration incantation.

Translating the cataa formulas commonly found in various yantras, from Thai into Khom

An important component of many yantras is the sacred cataa (incantation) believed to hold power and sanctity, placed within the yantra's grid, around its border, or among its other elements. The cataa written into a yantra is usually rendered mostly in Khom script. Here we take cataa that ancient masters commonly wove into yantra designs of various forms and translate them from Thai script into Khom script, so that students can compare, study, and understand the coded formulas found within yantra images, and so that this also serves as practice in reading and combining Khom letters. The content is as follows.

Buddha-Virtue Cataa (คาถาพระพุทธคุณ)

[Each line below is given in Khom script above its Thai transliteration.] Itipi so bhagavā (here spelt กะคะวา) arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇa- ("Thus indeed is the Blessed One: worthy, perfectly self-awakened, endowed with knowledge and conduct,") sampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisa-dhamma-sārathi (as spelt on the page) ("well-gone, knower of the world, unsurpassed charioteer/trainer,") satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā ti ("teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.")

Explanatory Notes

This "Manual of Great Yantras" compiles yantras drawn from many old treatises. Anyone who takes up this manual to study should first set up a teacher's bowl (khan khru), in order to ask permission and speak a request for blessing to the teachers, the elder teachers, and the originators of the tradition who devised it according to custom — that is, arranging flowers, incense, a candle, and a teacher's bowl in the manner generally practiced, consisting of:

1. One face-washing bowl.

2. Four cones made of fresh banana leaf, each containing inside:

2.1 One lotus flower

2.2 One incense stick

7. Silver and gold candles.

The Teacher-Veneration Verses

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa. (recite 3 times)

Buddhaṃ jīvitaṃ yāva nibbānaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi (I go for refuge to the Buddha for life, until Nibbāna): itipiso bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā ti — the standard recollection of the Buddha's virtues: the Blessed One, the Worthy One, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct, the Well-Gone, the Knower of Worlds, the Unsurpassed Trainer of persons to be tamed, the Teacher of gods and humans, the Enlightened, the Blessed One.

Dhammaṃ jīvitaṃ yāva nibbānaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi (I go for refuge to the Dhamma for life, until Nibbāna): svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo sandiṭṭhiko akāliko ehipassiko opanayiko paccattaṃ veditabbo viññūhī ti — the Dhamma well-proclaimed by the Blessed One, visible here and now, timeless, inviting one to come and see, leading onward, to be known individually by the wise.

Saṅghaṃ jīvitaṃ yāva nibbānaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi (I go for refuge to the Sangha for life, until Nibbāna): supaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho ... — the community of the Blessed One's disciples who practice well, practice straightly, practice methodically, practice properly; namely the four pairs, the eight kinds of persons — this community of the Blessed One's disciples is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.

Worship of the Buddha [brings] great glory; worship of the Dhamma [brings] great wisdom; worship of the Sangha [brings] great prosperity; I offer highest homage to the Most Excellent of the Three Worlds.

This chant is a verse of homage to the Triple Gem. In a medium or large ceremony that also includes silver and gold candles, light the silver and gold candles first, and only then recite the verse of homage to the Triple Gem. After that, light incense and candles in homage to the teachers, placing them according to the usual offering arrangement, and then recite the invitation to the deities, as follows:

[Pali verse inviting the deities dwelling in the heavens, in the realms of sense-desire and form, on mountain peaks, in the sky, in celestial mansions, on islands, in kingdoms, villages, young thickets, houses and grounds, and every locality — spirits of water and land, yakkhas, gandhabbas, and nāgas — to draw near and hear the sage's exposition: "It is now the time to hear the Dhamma, venerable ones; it is now the time to hear the Dhamma, venerable ones; it is now the time to hear the Dhamma, venerable ones."]

When inscribing a yantra, at the moment of inscription one must compose the mind into deep, unwavering concentration. The hand doing the inscribing must be steady, with the mind set straight. Every kind of yantra has a formula prescribed to govern it; when drawing the lines of a yantra, the drawing must run to completion exactly with the formula, and it is required that it be written in a single continuous stroke without stopping, for otherwise it will not be potent — because stopping partway through is considered spoiled. If one is not to spoil it, the formula must be continued.

The single most important prohibition is this: characters or numerals must never be allowed to overlap or cross the lines of the yantra. Such a yantra is called a "blind yantra" and cannot be used. ยันต์เมื่อลงเสร็จแล้วจำเป็นต้องปลุกเสกทุกยันต์ บางยันต์ก็วางคาถาปลุกเสกไว้ให้ หรือบางทีวางสูตรการลงกำกับไว้ให้ด้วย ยันต์เช่นว่านี้ต้องกระทำไปตามสูตรที่บังคับไว้ และเมื่อเสร็จแล้วก็ต้องปลุกเสกตามคาถาที่วางไว้กำกับนั้น ส่วนยันต์บางยันต์วางไว้แต่คาถาสำหรับปลุกเสก สูตรที่จะใช้ลงนั้นมิได้วางไว้ นั่นก็คือหมายความว่าให้ลงตามสูตรดังกล่าวแล้ว และบางยันต์มิได้วางไว้เลยทั้งสูตรลงและคาถาปลุกเสกยันต์เช่นนี้ จำเป็นจะต้องเลือกกาถาปลุกเสกเอง โดยกำหนดคาถานั้นให้เหมาะสมกับยันต์ เช่น ยันต์ทางคงกระพันให้เลือกเอาคาถาทางคงกระพันปลุกเสก ทางเมตตาหรือทางคุ้มกันก็ให้เอาคาถาเมตตาและคาถาคุ้มกันปลุกเสก หรือจะใช้คาถาพระพุทธคุณ พระธรรมคุณ พระสังฆคุณ คาถาพระเจ้า ๑๖ พระองค์ คาถาพระเจ้า ๔ พระองค์ กับบทใดบทหนึ่งปลุกเสกก็ได้ ยันต์บางชนิด ท่านบอกไว้แต่เพียงว่าให้ปลุกเสกด้วยตัวเอง หมายความว่าให้ถอดเอาคาถาที่ใช้ลงในยันต์นั้นนำเอามาปลุกเสก

ส่วนวิธีการปลุกเสกนั้น สำคัญอยู่ที่การวางกระแสจิต ถ้าประสงค์จะให้เป็นไปทางอยู่คงกระพันชาตรี ต้องวางอารมณ์ให้แข็งแกร่ง ไม่ย่นย่อต่อศาสตราอาวุธทั้งปวง ยิ่งทำกำลังใจให้แข็งได้เท่าไร ความศักดิ์สิทธิ์ก็จะยิ่งเพิ่มขึ้น ถ้าจะให้เป็นทางเมตตามหานิยมก็ต้องปรุงกระแสจิตให้อ่อนหวานเยือกเย็น การบริกรรมปลุกให้ดำเนินตามวิธีการภาวนาคาบ กระทำจนดวงจิตเป็นสมาธิ และในการปลุกเสกนี้ควรดำเนินไปตามหลักการของกสิณ กระทำให้เกิดอุคหนิมิตร และปฏิภาคนิมิตรบังเกิดปีติโดยลักษณะอาการต่างๆ เป็นต้นว่า เกิดขนพองสยองเกล้า เลขยันต์ที่ลงนั้นจะบรรลุถึงความศักดิ์สิทธิ์อย่างเต็มที่ อนึ่ง ในการปลุกเสกนั้น แม้ท่านจะวางคาถาให้ปลุกเสกด้วยบทใดบทหนึ่งก็ดี พึงอย่าลืมเอาธาตุเข้าประสมปลุกเสกด้วยเป็นอันขาด ให้พิจารณาดูกองธาตุอันควรจะใช้ให้เหมาะสมกับการนั้น

Consecration (Pluk Sek)

Once a yantra has been inscribed, every yantra must be consecrated. Some yantras come with a consecration incantation already provided; sometimes the formula for the inscribing is provided as well, governing it. Such a yantra must be made according to the formula prescribed, and once completed it must be consecrated according to the incantation laid down to govern it. As for some yantras, only the consecration incantation is provided, while the formula to be used for inscribing is not provided; this simply means one should inscribe using the formula already stated. And some yantras provide neither the inscribing formula nor the consecration incantation at all; such a yantra requires one to choose a consecration incantation oneself, selecting an incantation appropriate to the yantra. For example, for a yantra of the invulnerability path, choose an invulnerability incantation to consecrate it; for the path of loving-kindness or protection, use a loving-kindness incantation or a protection incantation to consecrate it; or one may use the Incantation of the Buddha's Virtues, the Dhamma's Virtues, the Sangha's Virtues, the Incantation of the 16 Lords Buddha, or the Incantation of the 4 Lords Buddha — any one of these verses may serve to consecrate it. For some kinds of yantra, it is stated only that it should be "self-consecrated," meaning that the incantation used to inscribe that yantra should itself be drawn out and used to consecrate it.

As for the method of consecration, what matters most is the placement of the mental current. If the intent is for the path of bodily invulnerability, one must set one's mood to be firm and unyielding, undaunted before any weapon or blade whatsoever; the firmer one can make one's resolve, the greater the potency will become. If the intent is for the path of great charm and popularity (metta mahaniyom), one must instead compose the mental current to be gentle, sweet, and cool. The muttering recitation for consecration should proceed according to the method of meditative recitation, carried out until the mind reaches concentration; and in this consecration one should proceed according to the principles of kasina meditation, bringing about the acquired sign (uggaha-nimitta) and the counterpart sign (patibhaga-nimitta), giving rise to rapture (piti) through various manifestations — such as, for instance, the hair standing on end. Only then will the numerals inscribed in the yantra attain their potency in full. Furthermore, in this consecration, even where an incantation is provided for consecrating with one verse or another, one must never forget to blend in the elements (thatu) as part of the consecration as well; one should consider which grouping of elements is appropriate and fitting to the purpose at hand.

The Elements (Thatu)

Another element that forms a crucial component in the matter of mantras and incantations is "the elements," as the ancient teachers have stated as follows:

"Siddhikariya: if one wishes to study the consecrating of anything at all, one must first come to know the elements — only then can one accomplish every kind of working." There is an explanatory saying on this, as follows: these elements form the great foundation; devas, Indra, Brahma, human beings, and all things in this world are able to live and endure only because of the elements. If the elements could not be obtained, these three worlds would perish entirely. For this reason, the elements have been praised as the great foundation; whoever comes to know the elements is greatly blessed indeed.

Na is the water element (apo-dhatu), the element of water that nourishes the body and the mind. Mo is the earth element (pathavi-dhatu), the element of earth. Phut is the fire element (tejo-dhatu), the element of fire, which warms and preserves the body. Tha is the wind element (vayo-dhatu), the element of wind, which envelops life. Ya is the space element (akasa-dhatu); wherever there is space, there is a place of refuge indeed. Na corresponds to the Lord Buddha Kakusandha, water element, 12. Mo corresponds to the Lord Buddha Konagamana, earth element, 21. Phut corresponds to the Lord Buddha Kassapa, fire element, 6. Tha corresponds to the Lord Buddha Gotama, wind element, 7. Ya corresponds to the Lord Buddha Sri Ariya Metteyya, space element, 10.

Taken together, these total 56, the virtues of the Buddhas. If this total of 56 virtues of the Buddhas is divided in two, it gives 28; adding in the 10 of the space element gives 38, which is the virtue of the Dhamma. If the 56 virtues of the Buddhas are divided into four parts, this gives 14, which is the virtue of the Sangha. So: the virtue of the Buddha is 56, the virtue of the Dhamma is 38, the virtue of the Sangha is 14; together these total 108. (The number 108 is held to be a potent number — for instance, reciting a consecration 108 times is considered a consecration that produces sacred potency.) Let all people come to know the virtues of the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Sangha in this way.

On Making Things Pure

When one is about to write or inscribe any numerical yantra, one must first purify oneself and the various materials to be used in writing or inscribing it, because it is believed that materials brought to a yantra may have passed through something inauspicious, carry defilement, contain impure elements, or have been obtained improperly against morality — and so are unfit to be used for so sacred a yantra. Yantra of the Four Lord Buddhas

[yantra: three tall flame-like spires rising from three circular medallions, each bearing a syllable, joined by crossing lines down to two further medallions below -- five medallions in all, arranged in a triangular formation]

This yantra may be inscribed onto a takrut amulet or onto a protective cloth. When inscribing it, do so inside the ordination hall (uposatha), with offerings arranged including a pig's head and a bai-si tray, and have monks chant the Chaiyanto verses over a Buddha image as well; only then will it be truly auspicious. Inscribe and consecrate it with this verse:

"Homage to the god free of human bonds, worshipped by gods and men; homage to him who is beyond desire's snares, I pay homage to the well-gone Victor" -- inscribe the syllable na. "Delusion that has cut off arrogance, delusion proclaimed as ultimate truth, delusion of omniscient knowledge, I pay homage to him who has conquered delusion" -- inscribe the syllable mo. "The Buddha, buddha among buddhas, buddha-nature, what the Buddha has spoken and declared, arrived at buddhahood, I pay homage to him who has conquered as Buddha" -- inscribe the syllable phut. "The elements of all phenomena, the arising of elements as declared, the elements of all who have attained Nibbana, I pay homage to him who has become the elements" -- inscribe the syllable tha. "Where patience is ordained, where the Dhamma bears wisdom, where the Sangha has arrived, I pay homage to that ground" -- inscribe the syllable ya.
