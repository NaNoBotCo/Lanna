What this is

This is the catch-all: 369 witnesses the source cataloguers could not place. The raw labels say it outright — 207 "Undetermined," 96 "Miscellany," 41 "Other," 15 blank, and ten rows where the label field has simply been filled with the item's own Thai title because no label existed to give it.

The temptation with a bucket like this is to apologise for it and move on. Don't. Go and look inside it. What is actually in here turns out to be a much more interesting document than the tidy genres are — because a residue tells you what a classification scheme cannot see, and this residue contains, among other things, the region's own older catalogues, a rain-making manual, the rice-mother, seventeen copies of a text nobody has identified, and 1,497 fully transcribed pages.

The census — what is really in the bucket

216 of the 369 carry no title at all, and they break down usefully:

  • 95 are a bare Untitled / ไม่ปรากฏ
  • 70 read no title (6474), no title (5919) — known only by an accession number
  • 4 are blank
  • 46 are Untitled (…) with a cataloguer's parenthetical guess inside, and those 46 are the gold

That last group is where the reclamation work is, because the guesses are specific. Five read Untitled (Khatha). Four read Untitled (Nyan lae katha) — "yantra and spell." Three read Untitled (Katha tang tang), "assorted katha." Others: Untitled (Sut thon, Nyan lae katha); Untitled (Nyan, katha lae withi kam sang ban) — yantra, katha, and the method for building a house; Untitled (Withi sang ban, Wan tat lep lae withi puca phi hua luang) — how to build a house, the day for cutting your nails, and how to make offerings to the great head-spirit. In all, 27 titles in this bucket name a katha or a yan. Those belong with magic and ritual, the katha and yantra pages, not here.

The rest of the bucket resolves into recognisable families. Not one of them is miscellaneous; they are just awkward.

The rite-bundles, which are the real reason this bucket exists

Twenty-four items have "และอื่นฯ" or "ฯลฯ" — "and others" — in their titles. These are not manuscripts with a subject. They are compilations, often twenty works deep, and the cataloguer's honest response to a compilation is to file it nowhere. Some are enormous. Ms 5079 at Wat Ban Ueam, Lampang, 156 pages of mulberry paper, contains: entering the names of monks and novices; the rules of the uposatha hall; the formal enquiry about a site for building a relic-stupa and an ordination hall; the enchanting of sand and of herb-water; the ordination of a white-robed female renunciant — and then "and others." Ms 5524 at Wat Si Khom Kham, 120 pages, opens with สะเดาะเคราะห์ (deflecting misfortune) and ทำขวัญเจ้านาย (calling the khwan of a lord), then the New Year offering of flowers, candles and scented water.

Trace the recurring components and this bucket is full of the archive's own core subjects, unlabelled: ถอน thon, the withdrawal rites, in five titles — ms 254 at Wat Phra That Chang Kham in Nan lists thon for a dead novice, a dead man, a dead woman, and everyone who died a bad death, which is precisely the thon corpus; สืบชาตา suep cata, the life-extension rite, inside ms 5691 at Wat Saen Mueang Ma; ปูชาท้าวทังสี่, worship of the Four Great Kings, in three; and the ubiquitous ส่งเคราะห์, sending off misfortune.

Inference — the bundle is the natural unit of Lanna practical religion and the worst possible unit for a subject catalogue. Half the contents of these compilations would sit comfortably in liturgy and chanting if they had been copied out one work at a time; bound together, they sit here. A genre scheme assumes one manuscript, one subject. These books were assembled by a working monk to hold everything he might be asked to perform, in the order he happened to acquire it. "Undetermined" is not a verdict on the contents. It is a structural failure — the scheme has no slot for a working repertoire.

Rain, rice, and the fields

A small cluster here is agricultural, and it is unlike anything else in the archive.

Ms 5791 at Wat Si Khom Kham, Phayao, 55 pages: วิธีขอน้ำฟ้าสายฝนกับพระยาแถน — the method for asking rain-water from Phaya Thaen — followed immediately by วิธีห้ามฝน, the method for making rain stop, and then ส่งสัตทังหลายที่มาบีบเบียนไร่นา, the sending-away of the creatures that come to oppress the fields. One book: turn it on, turn it off, and clear the pests.

Ms 5498 at the same temple runs ขอฝนจิมพระเจ้าห้าตน (petitioning the Five Lords for rain), แมงบ้งตกท่งนา (the caterpillars that fall on the paddy), and แปงช้างเผีก (making a white elephant). Ms 5692 at Wat Saen Mueang Ma is หนังสือวิธีกาน กานทำนา และปลูกเข้า — the book of method: how to make a rice field and plant rice. And the rice-mother herself is here in five witnesses: ธัมม์พระโปสบ / ธรรมพระโพสพ, the dhamma of Phra Phosop the rice deity, at Wat Nam Dip in Lampang and twice at Wat Mueang Mo in Phrae; and ย่าขวัญข้าว, "Grandmother Rice-khwan," at Wat Phra Chao Lan Thong in Chiang Rai (dated 1967) and Wat Mongkhonnimit in Chiang Mai.

Inference — this is subsistence agriculture's liturgy, and there is no genre for it in this scheme. It is not canonical, not chronicle, not law, not quite magic and ritual. It is the muang keeping itself fed, and it fell straight through the grid. It is also, for a project documenting a living tradition, some of the most alive material in the archive: rice-khwan rites are still performed.

Seventeen copies of something nobody has identified

The title หอรมาน / หอระมาน — Haraman — appears seventeen times across the collection. It is filed in four different genres: 8 here in Other, 7 in poetry and literature, 1 in astrology, 1 in chronicle. The witnesses run from Chiang Mai to Chiang Rai to Phayao to Lamphun to Phrae, dated between 1852 and 1973 where dated at all, and at least two are labelled ผูกต้น and ผูก 2 — first fascicle, second fascicle — so it is a long work in parts.

Seventeen copies across five provinces, in fascicles, and four cataloguers could not agree what kind of thing it is. Inference — a text with that distribution is not marginal; it is popular. The most likely reading is that this is Hanuman, and that the Lanna Rama-story is sitting in this archive under a spelling nobody's genre scheme recognised — note ms 726 at Wat Kam Ko, เจ้ารามะ ลังกาสิบหัว, "Lord Rama and the ten-headed one of Lanka," 285 pages in Shan on mulberry paper, dated 1989, also filed here. But that is a hypothesis, and it is exactly the kind of hypothesis a single afternoon of transcription would settle. This is the highest-value identification target in the archive.

Strays from the canon

Nine titles here are unmistakably commentarial Pali that belongs with the canon: Ṭīkā abhidhammatthasaṅgaha at Wat Lai Hin Luang; Untitled (Sāratthapakāsinī); Untitled (Manorathapūraṇī ṭīkā); Untitled (Appadāna Atthakathā); Ekasīti dutiyavaṇṇanā at Wat Phra Sing Woramahawihan, dated 1808. Four more are the Thūpavaṃsa, the chronicle of the relic-stupas — ms 6669 and ms 6670 at Wat Klang in Phrae as phuk 1–4 and 5–7, ms 6686 and ms 6688 at Wat Ratanaram in Chiang Mai as phuk 1–3 and 8–10, both sets dated in the 1870s–80s. Inference — a fascicle-set catalogued as separate accessions loses the work; the Thūpavaṃsa is here twice, complete-ish, and invisible, because no single row says so.

There is also a distinct block of Tham Lao Pali scholastic texts at Wat Sung Men, all dated 1836 — Yojanā aṭṭhasālinī, Aṭṭhakathā puggalapaññatti, Lokasaṇṭhāna jotaratanagaṇṭhi (a cosmology, in three copies), Sotabbamālinī, Abbhantara nidāna. Sixteen witnesses, all at Wat Sung Men, all in Tham Lao, all dated 1836, all dumped in "Undetermined." Inference — that is not a judgement about sixteen texts. It is one cataloguing session where an unfamiliar hand slowed somebody down.

The catalogues of catalogues

And the best thing in the bucket. Twelve witnesses here are lists of manuscript titles — ชื่อธัมม cue tham, "the names of the dhamma-texts," and เส้นธัมม์ sen tham, the roll of them. Nine of the twelve are at Wat Sung Men:

  • ms 2584, ชื่อธัมมะเมืองหลวงพระบาง — the names of the dhamma-texts of Luang Prabang, held in Phrae; and ms 4000, a second copy, at Wat Ban Pang in Lamphun
  • ms 2585, เส้นชื่อธัมส้างในเมืองน่าน — the roll of texts made in Nan
  • ms 668 at Wat Na Pang, Nan, หนังสือชื่อธัมมอันส้างในเมืองเชียงใหม่ และเมืองน่าน — the book of the names of texts made in Chiang Mai and Nan, 23 pages
  • ms 2582, เส้นธัมม์เมืองละพูร — the roll of Lamphun's texts
  • ms 6927, เส้นธรรมเมืองลี้ — the roll of Mueang Li's texts
  • ms 2587, ไม่ปรากฏ (เส้นรายชื่อธัมม์ต่างๆ) — an untitled list of assorted dhamma-titles

A monastery in Phrae kept a written inventory of what Luang Prabang had, and of what had been copied in Nan, and of what Lamphun held. That is a union catalogue, maintained by hand, across a river and a border, two centuries before this project existed. Inference — these twelve manuscripts are the direct ancestors of the database you are reading. A modern cataloguer met them, could not decide what subject a list of subjects has, and filed them under Undetermined — which is either the funniest or the saddest sentence on this page, and is certainly the most useful, because a nineteenth-century title-roll is a checklist against which present-day survival can be measured. Read them and you learn what has been lost.

And 1,497 pages that are already transcribed

Ten of the 369 rows are not palm leaf at all. They are contributed modern printed treatises whose genre field simply repeats their Thai title, because the normaliser had nothing to map them to: the three volumes of Kammatthana Practice of Luang Por Ruesi Ling Dam, Setting Up Ceremonies, Ancient Edition, Conducting Religious Rituals, Complete, the Life of Luang Por Thob and the Katha He Used, Chinese Arts for Beauty and Wealth, Training the Power of the Mind volumes one and two, and Teaching Samadhi and the Use of Mind-Power.

Between them they carry 1,497 digested pages with transcription and translation — the single largest body of readable text sitting under any genre label in this collection. The bucket that is supposed to be the archive's blind spot contains its best-lit corner. Inference — these ten need re-filing into magic and ritual and the meditation material, and the re-filing is a ten-row update, not a research project. Do it, and this bucket loses its most misleading rows overnight.

One manuscript, read closely

If you want to see the whole problem in a single object, open ms 5076, mulberry paper, Wat Ban Ueam, Lampang, 29 pages — one of only a handful of non-printed items in this archive with page-level transcription.

Page 1 is not the manuscript. It is the survey card, photographed with it: a printed form from the Lanna palm-leaf preservation project, filled in by hand, with the Thai Government–Federal Republic of Germany sponsorship line and the Chiang Mai University arts-and-culture centre at the foot, and a microfilming date in BE 2531 (1988). It records the province (Lampang), the temple, the script (ไทยยวน), the languages (Tai Yuan and Pali), the condition ("good"), the conservation done ("not repaired, rewrapped in new cloth, cleaned") — and, at line 3, the subject the surveyor wrote down:

โหราศาสตร์, ไสยศาสตร์ — astrology, saiyasat.

The card says astrology and esoteric science. The database says Other. And at line 4 the surveyor has ticked the box that explains the whole discrepancy: หมวด 20 — รวมหลายหมวด, "category 20: several categories combined."

Page 2 is the scribe's own contents list, and it is a menu of exactly the material this archive exists for: tua khuet and sappa khuet and the withdrawal of khuet (inauspicious contamination); the withdrawal of a vihara; worship of the Four Kings; two sendings-off of misfortune; the five avatars of the Lord; a white horse and a red one; a candle-yantra. Page 3 gives page numbers for each, so the scribe indexed his own book. Then the manuscript itself, badly rotted, yielding a Pali saṅgha formula, a scatter of protective syllables, several grids of Lanna numerals — and, on page 6, one legible clause: ปาสาโท ว่า ปราสาท, pāsādo means prasat. A nissaya gloss, the word-by-word construing format described on the grammar page, turning up in a ritual bundle.

Inference — nothing about ms 5076 is undetermined. Its subject was determined in 1988, in ink, on the card that is bound with it. It was filed as Other because it has six subjects, and the field that would have carried them holds one value. Every mulberry-paper bundle in this bucket is likely to have a card like this. The cheapest reclamation pass in the entire project is not reading manuscripts — it is reading the survey cards already photographed as page one.

The shape of the collection

190 of 369 are palm leaf (51%), 148 are mulberry paper (40%), seven khoi, 24 unrecorded. And within the untitled 216, the skew is sharper: 118 mulberry against 80 leaf. Paper is the fingerprint of the unclassifiable.

An earlier profile of this genre called it "the only genre where paper rivals palm-leaf." That is not right, and the correction sharpens the point. Astrology runs 295 paper against 85 leaf — paper dominates there — and magic and ritual runs 140 paper against 117 leaf. The settled genres do not all run 88–97% leaf; the scholastic ones do (the canon 93%, grammar 97%, law 93%). Inference — the real division is not classified against unclassified. It is archived against used. Palm leaf is prepared for permanence; phap sa folding paper is what you write on when you expect to consult it, amend it, and carry it to a house where somebody is ill. Other, astrology and magic-ritual cluster together on paper because they are all the same kind of book: working documents. That is why they resist a subject scheme — and it is why the paper share here is a positive signal, not a defect. Sort this bucket by material and the top of the list is the living tradition.

It is also the most diverse slice of the collection on every axis but Tham Lanna. Scripts: 311 Tham Lanna, but also 17 Tham Lao, 17 Thai, 11 Shan, 9 Tham Lue. Languages: thirteen distinct values, including Pali-and-Tai-Lue (9), Pali-and-Shan (7), and a lone monolingual Tai Lue and a lone monolingual Shan. Geography is flat and tilted away from the usual centre: 58 with no province at all, then Phayao 56, Nan 54, Phrae 43, Lampang 41, Chiang Mai 41, Chiang Rai 32, Lamphun 30. By temple: 58 unattributed, then Wat Sung Men 35, Wat Phra Koet 19, Wat Lai Hin Luang 19, Wat Si Khom Kham 17.

Inference — the border scripts pool here because a Tham-Lanna-centric scheme finds them hard to read, and the flat geography follows from that: this is where the edges of the Lanna world land. There is a measurable version of this. The EFEO collection contributes 45 of its 377 manuscripts to this bucket — 11.9% — against the DLNTM catalogue's 314 of 6,582, or 4.8%. A manuscript from the smaller, more heterogeneous source is two and a half times more likely to end up unclassified. The bucket is partly a measure of which catalogue a thing came through.

Notes

  • Read it as a work queue, in this order. (1) Re-file the ten printed treatises — a ten-row fix that removes 1,497 transcribed pages from a bucket labelled "unread." (2) Photograph-read the survey cards on page one of the paper bundles; ms 5076 shows they carry a subject line already. (3) Reclaim the 27 katha/yan titles into magic and ritual and the nine ṭīkā/aṭṭhakathā titles into the canon. (4) Identify Haraman.
  • Twelve rows here are catalogues. The cue tham and sen tham lists are a nineteenth-century union catalogue of the northern and Lao libraries. They should be transcribed before anything else in this bucket, because they are the only documents in the archive that can tell you what is missing.
  • The metadata is the thinnest anywhere, and that is the point. Only 82 of 369 carry a date (22%, the lowest of any genre), and 58 have no temple and 58 no province. Never cite provenance or date statistics from this bucket as findings about a tradition; they are a measure of what has not been examined. (An earlier profile gave those two figures as 48; on re-query they are 58.)
  • Bundles need a different data model. Twenty-four titles here name multiple works explicitly, and the real number is far higher. One manuscript, one genre field, is the wrong shape for a working repertoire. Until a witness can carry several subjects, this bucket will keep refilling — see the knowledge graph for where multi-subject linkage would actually live.
  • It should shrink, and it is legitimate to say so. A healthy archive sees this genre lose rows as material is read and reclaimed. Its size is a to-do list, and the goal is to empty it — but the paper it holds, and the border scripts, and the rice-mother, are not a backlog. They are the part of the north that the categories were never built to hold.
