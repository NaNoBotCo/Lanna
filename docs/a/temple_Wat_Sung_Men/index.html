What this is

Wat Sung Men (วัดสูงเม่น), in Sung Men district, Phrae, is the keystone of the whole collection. One surviving monastic library, on its own, supplies 1,780 of the archive's 6,990 witnesses — better than one manuscript in four. It is by an enormous margin the single largest provenance in the corpus; the next-largest, Wat Lai Hin Luang in Lampang, holds 375. Understanding this one library's shape is the precondition for reading almost every other distribution in this wiki correctly.

It is also, on the evidence of its own dated leaves, the site of something closer to an industrial event than a gradual accumulation.

The story, first — the year 1836

Sort Wat Sung Men's 1,383 dated manuscripts by year and the curve does not look like a library. It looks like a campaign.

In จุลศักราช 1198 — 1836 CE — this temple dated 404 manuscripts. Four hundred and four fascicles, all carrying the same single year, from one monastery. That is more manuscripts in one year than most temples in this archive hold in total. It is not an isolated spike, either: 1833 gives 80, 1834 gives 87, 1837 gives 84, and 1842 gives another 144. Take the twenty years from 1826 to 1845 and they account for 1,060 witnesses — sixty percent of everything the temple holds. Two decades of one monastery's life produced the largest single block of manuscripts in this collection.

What was copied in that year is as telling as how much. The 404 break down as 233 canonical, 77 grammar and lexicography, 45 jātaka, 24 chronicle, and small numbers of everything else. That is not a random harvest of whatever manuscripts came to hand. It is a curriculum: scripture, plus the apparatus needed to read scripture, plus the story-literature a preacher works from. Somebody ordered a library built, decided what a complete one consisted of, and got it written.

Tradition holds — Wat Sung Men's fame in Phrae rests on exactly this. The temple is remembered for a great scripture-gathering effort under a nineteenth-century abbot of enormous local standing, remembered in Phrae as ครูบากัญจนอรัญญวาสีมหาเถร (Khruba Kanchana Aranyawasi), who is credited with organising expeditions to bring texts back for copying and with building the collection into a regional reference library. The temple is still known for ตากธรรม (tak tham), the periodic ceremonial "sunning of the scriptures" — the bundles carried out of the ho trai, aired against damp and insects, and paraded before they go back. Inference — whatever the details, the catalogue's own date-column independently corroborates the shape of the story: a concentrated, organised, well-resourced copying programme in the second quarter of the nineteenth century, not slow accretion.

The temple's own record notes that it was formally established in พ.ศ. 2383 (1840) and that it appears in the dhamma-scriptures under the name วัดสุ่งเม้นแก้วกว้าง — "Wat Sung Men of the broad jewel" — for the extent of its grounds; rulers and royal houses from across Lanna and Lan Chang came to venerate its Tripitaka. Inference — the founding date and the manuscripts do not agree, and the disagreement is informative: the temple holds 55 witnesses dated before 1800 — a full generation before it was formally established — and its oldest, ms 2601 and ms 1618 — two copies of the commentary on the Uparipaṇṇāsa — carry dates of 1549 and 1550, nearly three centuries earlier. A registration date records when an institution was recognised, not when its shelves began. The library is older than the temple's paperwork.

The Lan Chang seam

That "Lanna and Lan Chang" detail is not decoration — it is measurable on the shelf. Of the 1,780 witnesses, 380 are written in Tham Lao script, and 128 are catalogued as Pali-and-Lao in language. More than a fifth of this Phrae library is in the writing system of the Mekong kingdoms rather than the Tham Lanna of its own valley.

Inference — a library whose script profile is one-fifth Lao is not serving one valley. It is a regional destination — either drawing manuscripts and copyists from across the Mekong world, or supplying it, and most likely both. The temple's tradition of royal visitors from Lan Chang and the 380 Tham Lao leaves are the same fact, recorded once in memory and once in the catalogue.

What the apparatus tells you

The strongest evidence that this was a working scholastic library rather than a merit-making stockpile is what sits alongside the scriptures.

  • นิไส — nissaya, the Pali-vernacular interlinear gloss, where a Pali text is broken word by word and each word rendered into Northern Thai. The corpus holds 172; 110 are here. This is the teaching format: a text prepared so it can be construed in front of students.
  • สับ — sap, word-analysis and parsing manuals: 169 across the corpus, and the 1836 batch is thick with them (ms 817, ms 818, ms 820 and their neighbours).
  • สมันตปาสาทิกา — the Samantapāsādikā, the great commentary on the monastic Vinaya: 40 witnesses in the corpus, 33 of them at Wat Sung Men.
  • Grammar and lexicography — 154 witnesses, 40% of the archive's entire holding in that genre.

Inference — nobody commissions three dozen copies of a Vinaya commentary and 110 interlinear glosses to accrue merit by the leaf. That is a reference collection assembled for people who had to teach Pali and settle points of monastic discipline, and who needed the commentaries to hand while doing it. The 1836 campaign built a school's shelf.

There is a payoff here for the legal side of this archive. The Samantapāsādikā is where the tradition works out the twenty-five modes of theft (อวหารซาวห้า) that the Mangraisat cluster puts to work as criminal law — and Wat Sung Men holds both the commentary in 33 copies and a Mangraisat of its own, ms 2030. The scholastic apparatus and its secular application are shelved in the same building.

Its physical profile

A pristine scholastic archive: 99% palm-leaf (1,770 of 1,780), against just 5 on mulberry paper and 1 on khoi. 1,383 witnesses carry a date — 78% — far better recorded than the collection at large, spanning 1549 to 1971. The language tilt is equally distinctive: 476 monolingual Pali alongside 1,164 Pali-and-Lanna. Inference — nearly five hundred manuscripts with no vernacular in them at all is the signature of a library oriented to scripture and its scholastic apparatus rather than to local vernacular use, and it is the cleanest slice of the collection against which to calibrate what a fully-described witness looks like.

The tension that matters to the wichaa

Here is the crucial caveat for this project. Wat Sung Men is the biggest library in the collection and simultaneously one of the least relevant to the wichaa core. It holds only 14 of 388 astrology witnesses (~4%) and 13 of 276 magic and ritual (~5%), and just 27 of its 1,780 witnesses are flagged research-priority.

Set that against what it dominates: 1,014 of the archive's 2,350 canonical manuscripts (43%), 40% of the grammar, and 354 of 1,633 jātaka (22%). The scholastic survival bias runs exactly opposite to this archive's priorities. The great library preserved the canon, the grammar and the birth-stories — precisely the best-documented material, the texts that also survive in a dozen other collections — while the efficacious saiyasat is scattered thin across the smaller temples that nobody endowed.

The lesson is a coverage lesson: volume is not centrality. The corpus's centre of gravity and its centre of interest are two different places, and the gap between them is roughly the shape of this one building.

The genre that proves it

The clearest demonstration is customary law. Wat Sung Men holds 12 of the 134 law witnesses — under 9%, a fraction of its share of anything scholastic. And with the great library's thumb off the scale, look what the law distribution does: Phrae 23, Phayao 23, Nan 23, Lampang 23. Four provinces, an identical count each.

Inference — that is what a genuine regional signal looks like when no single survival is distorting it. Every müang kept its own law in roughly the same quantity because every müang had to decide cases. Where Wat Sung Men is absent, the evenness is real history; where it is present, the ranking is mostly a fact about one temple's luck. That comparison — the lumpy scholastic genres against the flat legal one — is the single most useful calibration this article can give you.

Its own twelve law books are worth naming, because they are an ordinary working set rather than anything grand: four of โลกวิไนย / โวหารโลกวิไน (Lokawinai, "worldly discipline" — ms 1855, ms 1857, ms 1858, ms 1859), a Mangraisat (ms 2030), a Thammasat rachasat (ms 2031), a Kadi lok kadi tham (ms 2032), the "lesser Anacak" (ms 2033), Nisai kadi lok (ms 3940, dated 1827), Wohan mulatantai (ms 1977), Khulupathet (ms 816), and ms 2496, Wohan kadi son khon — "cases that teach people."

Notes

  • Read every scholastic ranking against this baseline. Before treating a province or temple count in the canonical, grammar or jātaka genres as historically meaningful, subtract the Wat Sung Men effect. The place-counts in those genres are a preservation artifact, not a map of where the texts were produced.
  • A model of good cataloguing. Its 78% dated rate and clean palm-leaf profile make it the best-recorded slice of the collection.
  • Not a crawl priority, but a fixed point. Little here is at mortal risk, so it does not compete for the next crawl's attention — but as the largest and best-dated anchor it is the reference frame against which the thin, urgent, scattered wichaa material should be measured.
  • A dating question worth someone's time. Inference — 404 manuscripts sharing the single year CS 1198 is either a real concentrated campaign or a cataloguing convention applied in bulk to an undated batch. The distinction matters for every temporal claim on this site. Checking the colophons of a sample of the 1836 witnesses would settle it, and it is a cheap check.
  • Two neighbours, one district. Sung Men district also yields Wat Phra Luang, holder of ms 2642 — the corpus's only law-book titled for the levying of compensation — plus 25 witnesses catalogued under "Wat Phra Luang Sung Men" and 22 under the district name alone.
