You have the thing; you don't have its name. Answer the questions a sian would ask — in the sian's order — and meet the kinds it could be.

Why class comes first. A sale listing flattens every kind on this page to “amulet.” The one thing it almost never states is the first thing the tradition asks: is anyone home? A พระสมเด็จ and a กุมารทอง trade on the same page, in the same price band — and one is impersonal blessing you may adopt blind, while the other is a raised spirit whose history should travel with it. Class facts and keeping below come from the vault vocabulary (Tradition); counts and prices from the catalogue (Catalogue); the material-and-figure mapping that drives the picker is this page's own curation (Inference), and each card signs which it is.

A rectangular pressed tablet of sacred powder bearing a seated Buddha in an arch — the most copied, most collected amulet form there is. Power is the maker's barami and the consecration; nobody is home.

ขุนแผน

khun phaen · the Khun Phaen tablet

A pressed tablet in the classic arch — named for the epic's charm-master, and the one amulet whose material story matters as much as its form. Some are pure powder; some are worked with necromantic material, and the difference does not show on the surface.

✳ The sharpest seam in the whole register, measured not decreed: Buddha-amulet FORM, but many makings carry prai-kuman MATERIAL. Its upkeep is an amulet's; its provenance question is a spirit-object's. Ask what went into the batch.

The boy-spirit of Wat Chedi in Sichon, the biggest devotional wave since Jatukham — asked for lottery luck and repaid in firecrackers and toy soldiers. The amulet carries the devotion; the vow-keeping is the keeping.
