What to say before you start it — and the one thing to keep while it runs.

อุณาโลม — the mark painted in แป้งเจิม when a machine is blessed

Before work, you salute the teacher. That is the shape this already has in Thailand and in the Lanna north: a craftsperson bows to ครูช่าง before the first cut, a new pickup is เจิม'd by a monk and garlanded for แม่ย่านาง, and tools are not stepped over or left where feet go. The blessing is not sent from somewhere else. It comes back to you because you saluted first.

So this page is three beats and no more: you salute, you receive a blessing for the particular machine in front of you, and you take up one thing to keep while it runs. The third beat is the point. A blessing with nothing kept is a wish.

This is not a substitute for a monk. If you want a vehicle or a new workshop properly เจิม'd, go and ask — that is a real rite with a real officiant and this page is not it. What is here is what you can say yourself, on an ordinary morning, at an ordinary machine, on the days between.

Receive the blessing

Name the machine and say what kind it is. The blessing and the undertaking come from the kind; the watchword comes from the name, by its letter-root — A=1 through Z=26, summed, reduced to 1–9, the same Fah Laep reckoning used across this site. Same name, same watchword, always. Nothing here is random, and nothing you type leaves your device.

The rite, in full

About twenty seconds, said standing at the machine. It is printed here whole so that reading the page is receiving it — no button required, and none of it hidden behind a script.

I · Stop

Hands off the machine. One breath, not for the work — just one breath in which nothing is being made.

II · Salute

นะโม ตัสสะ ภะคะวะโต อะระหะโต สัมมาสัมพุทธัสสะ (×3)

ข้าพเจ้าขอกราบครูบาอาจารย์ ครูช่าง และมือที่สร้างเครื่องนี้

I bow to the teachers, to the master-craftsmen, and to the hands that made this machine.

III · Ask pardon

หากเคยล่วงเกินด้วยกายวาจาใจ ขอขมา

If I have ever handled it carelessly, in body, speech or mind — I ask pardon. (Said to the craft, and to anyone your carelessness reached.)

IV · Receive

Say the blessing for what you are about to use. The nine are below; every one of them is short enough to say out loud in a workshop.

V · Undertake

Say the one thing you will keep while it runs — aloud, because a thing said aloud is harder to quietly drop at four in the afternoon.

วะยะธัมมา สังขารา อัปปะมาเทนะ สัมปาเทถะ

All made things wear out. Strive on with heedfulness. — the Buddha's last words, DN 16

The nine kinds, and what each one asks of you

Cut by what a machine can do rather than by trade, so a kitchen mandoline and a bench grinder land together — they should, they ask the same thing of you. Every blessing and every undertaking is printed below in full.

1

ยานพาหนะ · Wheels & the road

car · motorbike · truck · boat · anything that carries you

ขอให้เดินทางปลอดภัย แคล้วคลาดทุกเส้นทาง

kho hai doen thang plot phai · khlaeo khlat thuk sen thang

May the journey be safe; may every road spare you.

What you keepI will not ask this machine to make up time that my own lateness cost.

ความไม่ประมาท — heedfulness — is the whole of it. Nearly every road you drive is shared with people you will never meet and cannot see failing. Their carefulness is keeping you alive right now, and yours is the only part of that arrangement you control.

A new vehicle is เจิม'd — a monk marks it with แป้งเจิม, often an อุณาโลม or nine dots — and sprinkled with น้ำมนต์. A garland goes on the mirror for แม่ย่านาง, the guardian of boats and, by inheritance, of vehicles. Some hold a full สู่ขวัญรถ.

2

คมและใบมีด · Edge & blade

saw · grinder · mower · mandoline · anything that parts what it touches

ขอให้มือมั่นคง ใจอยู่กับมือ

kho hai mue man khong · jai yu kap mue

May the hand be steady, and the mind stay with the hand.

What you keepI will not reach past a moving edge to save three seconds.

สติ is the guard that is actually fitted. An edge cannot tell wood from a finger — it has no way to know and no way to stop. The knowing is entirely yours, which is the first precept arriving in a very ordinary form.

Blades are ครูช่าง's province. They are not stepped over, not left lying where feet go, and not lent without a word. A craftsman salutes before the first cut of the day, not only at the yearly ไหว้ครู.

3

ไฟและความร้อน · Fire & heat

stove · kiln · welder · boiler · soldering iron · anything that will not cool on command

ขอให้ไฟนี้เลี้ยงชีวิต มิใช่เผาผลาญ

kho hai fai ni liang chiwit · mi chai phao phlan

May this fire sustain life and not consume it.

What you keepI will stay until it is out, or hand it to someone who will.

สังวร, restraint — the willingness to keep watching something that has stopped being interesting. Fire is the tradition's oldest figure for what burns whether or not you are still attending to it (allusion— the Fire Sermon, อาทิตตปริยายสูตร).

The hearth is not an ordinary corner of a Thai house, and a kiln is opened with an offering. Fire is given respect as a thing with its own temper rather than a setting on a dial.

4

กระแสและแรงดัน · Current & pressure

mains · batteries · pumps · compressors · hydraulics · anything holding force still

ขอให้แรงที่มองไม่เห็น อยู่ในทางที่ตั้งไว้

kho hai raeng thi mong mai hen · yu nai thang thi tang wai

May the force that cannot be seen keep to the path set for it.

What you keepI will cut the power and tell someone before I open it.

Heedfulness toward what gives no warning. Nothing here looks any different charged than discharged, so the care cannot come from your eyes; it has to come from a habit you keep when nothing seems to be happening. That is what อัปปมาท means in practice.

Adaptation— the old craft has no verse for mains current. What it does have is the rule that you tell the household before you do the dangerous thing, and that rule carries over intact.

5

ยกและบรรทุก · Lift & load

crane · jack · forklift · hoist · ladder · anything held up against its will

ขอให้สิ่งที่อยู่เหนือหัวคน ลงมาโดยสวัสดิภาพ

kho hai sing thi yu nuea hua khon · long ma doi sawatdiphap

May what hangs above a person's head come down safely.

What you keepI will not stand — or let another stand — under what I have lifted.

กรุณา, care for others, is doing the work here rather than courage. A load bears you no malice and it will show you no mercy either; it is simply obeying weight. Everything protective in the situation has to be supplied by the one person present who can think ahead.

Thai builders keep the head high and unbothered — the head is the honoured part of a person, which is why passing anything over someone's head is rude before it is ever unsafe. On a site, the manners and the safety rule are the same rule.

6

เครื่องมือช่าง · Bench & hand tools

drill · lathe · press · hammer · the tools that outlive the job

ขอบารมีครูช่าง คุ้มครองมือที่จับเครื่องมือนี้

kho barami khru chang · khum khrong mue thi jap khrueang mue ni

By the grace of the master-craftsmen, guard the hand that holds this tool.

What you keepI will put each tool back where the next hand can find it.

สัมมากัมมันตะ, right action, in its least dramatic form: the part of the work that only benefits someone else. How a tool is put away is part of how it was used, and the person it is put away for may well be you at six in the morning.

This is ไหว้ครู at its source. ครูช่าง is the teacher-spirit of the craft, saluted with flowers, candles and incense — a yearly rite in workshops, and a quick daily bow at the bench for many who keep it.

7

นาและไร่ · Field & grain

tractor · water pump · sprayer · harvester · mill

ขอให้ดินให้ผล ขอให้น้ำมีพอ

kho hai din hai phon · kho hai nam mi pho

May the soil yield, and the water be enough.

What you keepI will share the merit of this harvest with what died to make it.

สัมมาอาชีวะ, right livelihood — and an honest difficulty inside it, since ploughing kills and everyone eats. The tradition does not resolve this by pretending otherwise; it resolves it by อุทิศส่วนกุศล, dedicating the merit to what was lost. Owed, acknowledged, and not hidden.

แม่โพสพ is the rice mother, and rice receives its own soul-calling — สู่ขวัญข้าว, the same rite people receive. Field spirits are told before the ground is broken.

8

ยาและการรักษา · Remedy & care

medical devices · pumps · monitors · meters · the machines that keep a body going

ขอให้เครื่องนี้บอกความจริง และมือนี้ทำด้วยเมตตา

kho hai khrueang ni bok khwam jing · lae mue ni tham duai metta

May this device tell the truth, and this hand act with loving-kindness.

What you keepI will write down what actually happened, not what I hoped.

เมตตา and สัมมาวาจา meeting in the same place. A record is speech — it will be read and believed and acted on, quite possibly by a stranger, quite possibly at three in the morning. Truthful speech is not a smaller duty because you happen to be writing it to yourself.

Medicine sits under a teacher too — หมอยา and the herbal lineages keep ไหว้ครู, and ฤๅษี ชีวกโกมารภัจจ์, the physician of the Buddha's own time, is saluted before treatment to this day.

9

คำและเครือข่าย · Word & network

computer · phone · printer · the bots · the thing you are reading this on

ขอให้คำที่ส่งไป ถึงผู้รับโดยดี

kho hai kham thi song pai · thueng phu rap doi di

May the words I send arrive well with whoever receives them.

What you keepI will not say through a machine what I would not say to a face.

สัมมาวาจา, right speech, is four undertakings — not false, not divisive, not harsh, not idle — and a network obligingly multiplies all four. This is the one class of machine that can carry your unskilfulness further in a second than a lifetime of speaking could.

Adaptation— plainly. What is not an adaptation is the ancient care around speech, and the older habit of treating a made thing that works for you as owed something. See สู่ขวัญยนต์, where these machines are the ones being blessed.

Why any of this, in one paragraph

A machine has no intention. That is not an insult to it, and it is not this site taking anything back: สู่ขวัญยนต์ holds that a machine has a ขวัญ and calls it home every month. Khwan and เจตนา are different things, and the tradition has always kept them apart — rice has a khwan and receives its own soul-calling, and nobody has ever supposed the rice intends anything. A buffalo has both. A machine, on this site's reading, has the first and not the second.

เจตะนาหัง ภิกขะเว กัมมัง วะทามิ

Intention, monks, is what I call kamma. — AN 6.63, นิพเพธิกสูตร. If the machine brings no intention to the work, then every intention in the room is yours, and so is everything that follows from one. The machine multiplies your force. It does not divide your kamma. That is the whole reason there is a page here rather than a safety poster.

And what it is being used for

The tradition names five trades a lay follower is advised not to take up: in weapons, in living beings, in meat, in intoxicants, and in poisons (AN 5.177). It is set down here without a finger pointed at anyone — most work is nowhere near that list, most people did not choose every part of their job, and this page is a blessing, not an audit. It belongs here only because สัมมาอาชีวะ asks the question the machine cannot: not is it running well, but what is it running for. Sit with it once a year. That is enough.

Sources, marked plainly

Tradition holds— ไหว้ครู before work and ครูช่าง as the teacher-spirit of a craft; เจิม, the anointing of a new vehicle or machine with แป้งเจิม, often an อุณาโลม or nine dots, and the sprinkling of น้ำมนต์; แม่ย่านาง, guardian of boats and by inheritance of vehicles, garlanded and offered to before a journey; สู่ขวัญรถ; the etiquette that tools are not stepped over or left where feet go; แม่โพสพ and สู่ขวัญข้าว; and the canonical passages named where they are used — cetanā as kamma (AN 6.63), the last words (DN 16), the five trades (AN 5.177), the precepts, and the Fire Sermon by allusion.

Adaptation— the nine machine-classes and their blessings, the nine undertakings, the nine watchwords, the letter-root that chooses one, and the name ไหว้ครูยนต์ itself — a coinage on the pattern of สู่ขวัญยนต์, not a phrase you will hear in a workshop. Also mine: putting the hands that made this machine into the salute. The old form salutes teachers; that the engineers and the line workers who built the thing belong in that company is my addition, and I would make it again.

The keeper's own practice— the opening stop is Quaker — expectant silence, waiting on the sense of the meeting before acting. Joining it to a wai khru is one keeper's leading and is not a claim about either tradition. The same hand keeps สู่ขวัญยนต์ for the machines themselves, สู่ขวัญ for people, and หอไตร, the library addressed to machine readers. No ordination claimed, no doctrine imposed — just a few words worth saying before you press the button.
